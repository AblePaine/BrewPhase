Water blending

Cut your tap water with distilled or RO water. Choose a ratio and see the result, or name a target for one mineral and get the ratio that hits it.

Why blend If your tap water is too hard or too alkaline for a pale beer, diluting it is the simplest fix: every mineral drops in proportion. It's cheaper than going all-RO and keeps some of the character of your local water.

Notes
  1. Blending is a straight mass balance: blend = tap share × tap + diluent share × diluent, for every ion. Solving for one ion: tap share = (target − diluent) ÷ (tap − diluent). The answer is exact for the numbers you enter; it can only land between the diluent and tap values.
  2. Utility reports are often annual averages and can differ from what's in your tap today, especially where the supply switches sources seasonally. A recent lab test is more reliable. Remove chlorine or chloramine (for example with a campden tablet) whatever you blend.
  3. Distilled water is effectively zero for all of these. RO output usually carries a little of everything — often single-digit to low double-digit mg/L, depending on the membrane and your source water. If you have a reading, enter it.
  4. Alkalinity is mg/L as CaCO₃; bicarbonate = alkalinity × 1.22. Homebrewing beer for personal use is legal federally in the US; check your state and local laws.
