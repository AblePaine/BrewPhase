Notes
  1. How the salts are solved: Epsom salt is the only magnesium source, baking soda the only sodium source, and calcium chloride the only chloride source, so those three are fixed by your targets. Gypsum then supplies whatever sulfate Epsom didn't; chalk supplies whatever alkalinity baking soda didn't. Calcium comes from gypsum, calcium chloride and chalk together, so it's a result rather than a separate dial — the table shows both your target and what you'll actually get. Molar masses use the shared atomic-weight table and the hydrated salt forms named above.
  2. Alkalinity is entered as mg/L as CaCO₃. Bicarbonate (HCO₃⁻) ppm = alkalinity × 61.016 ÷ 50.043, valid when water pH is below about 8.3, which is true for water built this way.
  3. Chalk (CaCO₃) is contested. It barely dissolves in plain water, and even stirred into the mash it typically delivers noticeably less alkalinity than theory — estimates of how much less vary widely. Many brewers use baking soda instead when they need alkalinity. Its result here is the full theoretical value.
  4. Starting profiles are common homebrew starting points, not rules. Adjust to taste.
  5. Weigh salts on a scale that reads to 0.1 g or better; teaspoon conversions vary too much with grind and packing to be exact. Homebrewing beer for personal use is legal federally in the US; check your state and local laws.
